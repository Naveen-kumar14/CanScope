## Scene 2: Identify the hardware

**Step 8 - ESP32 pin locator**

- `00:05:20.469` Hold each ESP32 with the components up and the USB connector pointing left.
- `00:05:27.292` Gate G1: read the marking on the metal shield. It must say ESP-WROOM-32, not WROVER. On a WROVER, GPIO sixteen and seventeen are used by memory and cannot be CAN pins.
- `00:05:42.945` Gate G2: read all thirty pin labels. Pin one of each header is the pin next to the USB connector.
- `00:05:51.728` The top row in these diagrams is the VIN side. The bottom row is the 3V3 side.
- `00:05:59.157` If your labels are in a different order, stop and send a photo. Do not count pins from the drawing.
- `00:06:07.220` Pins shaded dark red are flash, strapping or USB serial pins. CANScope never uses them.
**Step 9 - Transceiver identity**

- `00:06:17.568` This is the SN65HVD230 transceiver, marked VP230 on the chip.
- `00:06:25.571` Gate G4: check the chip marking, and check that the module's supply pin is labelled 3V3 or VCC with no five volt marking. The chip runs from three point zero to three point six volts.
- `00:06:40.643` CTX, also called TXD or D, is the transmit input. It connects to the ESP32 transmit pin. This is not crossed like a UART.
- `00:06:54.326` CRX, also called RXD or R, is the receive output. It connects to the ESP32 receive pin.
- `00:07:04.915` Gate G5 checks the Rs mode pin. It must be pulled to ground for high-speed mode. If it is high, the chip is in standby and cannot transmit or acknowledge.
- `00:07:17.642` If your module exposes a pin labelled RS, S or STB, wire it to ground. Leave Vref unconnected.
**Step 10 - Onboard termination**

- `00:07:28.546` Gate G6: many of these modules already have a 120 ohm resistor between CAN high and CAN low.
- `00:07:36.988` With the module unpowered and connected to nothing, measure CAN high to CAN low.
- `00:07:42.987` Above ten kilo-ohms means no onboard terminator. About 120 ohms means there is one.
- `00:07:51.335` If the Node one or Node three module has one, leave out that end's external resistor.
- `00:07:58.029` If the Node two module has one, stop. That resistor must be removed, so send a photo first.
- `00:08:07.177` The aim is exactly two terminators on the whole bus.
**Step 11 - OLED and LEDs**

- `00:08:13.121` Gate G7: read the four OLED labels. G N D, V C C, S C L, S D A is common, but some modules swap ground and V C C. Always connect by the printed name.
- `00:08:28.869` The OLED is powered from three volt three only, so its pull-ups keep the data lines at three volt three.
- `00:08:36.842` Gate G9: the LED's long leg is the anode. The short leg, next to the flat edge, is the cathode. Confirm with the meter's diode test.
**Step 12 - Buttons and resistors**

- `00:08:50.760` Gate G8: on a four-leg button, two diagonally opposite legs are always on different contacts, whichever way you turn it.
- `00:09:00.343` Check with the meter. The diagonal legs must be open when released and closed when pressed.
- `00:09:07.602` Gate G13: measure every resistor before you insert it. Colour bands are easy to misread.
**Step 13 - Buzzer and transistor**

- `00:09:27.788` Gate G10: the transistor is blocked. Different transistors have different leg orders. For example, the PN2222A is emitter, base, collector, but the BC547 is collector, base, emitter.
